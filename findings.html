Key findings

Every chart on this dashboard, this page included, answers a specific question about how 2025-2026 conditions departed from the fixed calendar-month climatology (see About for the full methodology) — computed live from the same JSON every other page uses, not a static image. Every value here is descriptive: a single year (or month) compared against its own baseline, never against another year.

How anomalous was 2025-2026 across the snow, water, and vegetation observing systems, product by product? That's the "By category" view on Compare variables — every product in a theme overlaid on one standardized-anomaly axis, observations solid / models dashed. How did physical anomalies emerge through the year, across every product at once? That's the Heatmaps section, same page.

How did each DJFM winter classify by snow-drought type?

Dierauer, Allen & Whitfield (2019, Water Resources Research) split below-normal snowpack winters by their precipitation and temperature departure into dry, warm-and-dry, and warm snow droughts. Each point is one DJFM winter (1991–2026), placed by its own anomaly relative to the fixed baseline — pick any two drivers to see how they relate; the color always reflects the Dierauer classification itself (temperature × precipitation × snowpack), not the two axes shown.

Descriptive only -- no product is combined with another. Source: code/11_context_SnowDroughtQuadrants_analyze.py's own classification table.
